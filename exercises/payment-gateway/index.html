Payment Gateway — Requirements Exercise

Design brief: Design a service that lets merchants accept payments from customers and settles funds through payment providers.

Functional Requirements

  • Merchants can initiate a payment charge for a specified amount, currency, and customer.
  • The system must support multiple payment methods (cards, bank transfers, digital wallets).
  • The system must route each transaction to an appropriate downstream payment processor or acquiring bank.
  • Customers can complete a payment and receive a clear success or failure outcome.
  • The system must support refunds, both full and partial, against a completed payment.
  • The system must guarantee that a retried payment request is not charged more than once (idempotency).
  • Merchants can query the status and history of their transactions.
  • The system must notify merchants of payment outcomes via webhooks or callbacks.
  • The system must support authorization and later capture as separate steps.
  • The system must handle chargebacks and disputes initiated by customers or banks.
  • The system must produce reconciliation records matching internal transactions with provider settlements.

Non-Functional Requirements

  • The system must process 10,000 transactions per second at peak.
  • Payment authorization latency should be under 2 seconds end to end (p99).
  • Availability target of 99.99% for the payment-processing path.
  • Financial records must be strongly consistent and durable, with no double-charging or lost transactions.
  • The system must comply with PCI-DSS and protect sensitive cardholder data in transit and at rest.
  • Every transaction must produce a complete, immutable audit trail for regulatory and dispute purposes.
  • The system must remain correct under retries, partial failures, and provider timeouts.
  • Fraud-relevant signals must be captured for each transaction to support risk evaluation.
  • The system must support multiple currencies and regional regulatory requirements.

Assumptions & Out of Scope

  • Assume merchants are already onboarded and KYC-verified; merchant onboarding flows are out of scope.
  • The internal fraud-scoring model itself is out of scope; only capturing signals is in scope.
  • Assume downstream processors and banks expose APIs for authorization and settlement.
  • Consumer-facing checkout UI design is excluded; focus is on the gateway service.
  • Tax calculation and invoicing are out of scope.
