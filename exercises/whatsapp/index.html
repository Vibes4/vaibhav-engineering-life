WhatsApp — Requirements Exercise

Design brief: Design a mobile messaging service that lets users exchange one-to-one and group messages in real time.

Functional Requirements

  • Users can send and receive one-to-one text messages in real time.
  • Users can create and participate in group chats with many members.
  • The system must show per-message delivery states: sent, delivered, and read.
  • Users can see the online/last-seen and typing status of their contacts (subject to privacy settings).
  • Users can send media attachments such as images, video, audio, and documents.
  • Messages sent while a recipient is offline must be delivered once they reconnect.
  • The system must preserve message ordering within a conversation as perceived by participants.
  • Users can register and be discoverable by phone number, syncing existing device contacts.
  • Users can make voice and video calls.
  • Message history must sync across a user's linked devices.
  • Users can receive push notifications for new messages when the app is backgrounded.

Non-Functional Requirements

  • Scale target: ~2B users and on the order of 100B messages exchanged per day.
  • Message delivery should feel instantaneous, with end-to-end delivery p99 under ~1 second when both parties are online.
  • Availability target of 99.99%; the service must remain usable during regional outages.
  • Messages must be end-to-end encrypted so that the platform cannot read message contents.
  • Undelivered messages must be durably retained until delivered (with a defined retention window).
  • Once delivered to all recipients, message payloads should not be retained server-side longer than necessary.
  • The system must operate reliably on low-bandwidth and intermittent mobile connections.
  • Group message fan-out must scale to large groups without degrading delivery latency.
  • The system must resist spam and abuse at the account and message-rate level.

Assumptions & Out of Scope

  • Assume each user is identified by a verified phone number; identity/KYC design is out of scope.
  • Broadcast channels, payments, and business/API messaging are excluded unless stated.
  • Assume clients handle local encryption key storage; key-exchange protocol internals are out of scope.
  • Long-term server-side archival of chat history beyond sync needs is excluded.
