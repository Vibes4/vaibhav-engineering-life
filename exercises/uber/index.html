Uber — Requirements Exercise

Design brief: Design a ride-hailing service that matches riders requesting trips with nearby available drivers.

Functional Requirements

  • Riders can request a ride from a pickup location to a destination.
  • The system must match a rider's request to a suitable nearby available driver.
  • Drivers can go online/offline and accept or decline incoming ride requests.
  • The system must continuously track and update driver locations while they are online.
  • Riders can see nearby drivers and an estimated time of arrival before requesting.
  • The system must provide an upfront fare estimate and compute the final fare at trip end.
  • Riders and drivers can track each other's live location during pickup and the trip.
  • The system must support surge/dynamic pricing during high demand.
  • Payment must be collected from the rider and settled to the driver after each trip.
  • Riders and drivers can rate each other after a completed trip.
  • The system must maintain trip history for both riders and drivers.
  • The system must handle cancellations by either party, with applicable policies.

Non-Functional Requirements

  • Scale target: millions of active drivers and tens of millions of trips per day globally.
  • Driver location updates arrive at high frequency (every few seconds per active driver).
  • Matching latency should be low, with a rider seeing a matched driver within a few seconds (p99).
  • Availability target of 99.99%; the platform is real-time and revenue-critical.
  • Location and matching must be strongly geo-consistent enough to avoid double-booking a driver.
  • Financial transactions (fares, payouts) must be accurate and auditable with no lost or duplicated charges.
  • The system must operate across many cities/regions and time zones with low regional latency.
  • Rider and driver location/PII must be protected and access-controlled.
  • The system must degrade gracefully under demand spikes (e.g., events, weather).

Assumptions & Out of Scope

  • Assume map, routing, and ETA data are available from an external maps provider.
  • Food delivery, freight, and other business lines are out of scope.
  • Assume driver onboarding/background checks exist; designing that pipeline is excluded.
  • Assume a payment gateway exists; card network integration internals are out of scope.
  • Multi-stop and scheduled/future rides are excluded unless stated.
