Amazon Cart — Requirements Exercise

Design brief: Design the shopping cart service for a large e-commerce platform that lets shoppers collect items and carry them through to checkout.

Functional Requirements

  • Users can add an item (with quantity and variant such as size/color) to their cart.
  • Users can update quantities, remove items, and clear the cart.
  • The system must persist a signed-in user's cart so it is available across sessions and devices.
  • The system must support guest carts and merge them into the user's cart upon sign-in.
  • The system must show current per-item price, subtotal, and applicable taxes/shipping estimates.
  • The system must reflect item availability and flag out-of-stock or price-changed items at view time.
  • Users can save items for later / move them to a wishlist and back into the cart.
  • The system must apply eligible promotions, coupons, and quantity limits to the cart.
  • The system must hand off the cart contents to the checkout/order flow to place an order.
  • The system must support multiple concurrent carts contexts where applicable (e.g., business vs personal).
  • The system must validate cart contents (stock, price, limits) at checkout initiation.

Non-Functional Requirements

  • Support hundreds of millions of active shoppers with tens of thousands of cart operations per second at peak (e.g., major sale events).
  • Add-to-cart and view-cart operations should respond within ~100–200 ms at p99.
  • Availability target of 99.99%; the cart must remain writable even during partial backend degradation.
  • A saved cart must be durable and must not lose items that were successfully added.
  • The cart should favor high availability, tolerating brief inconsistency (e.g., stale price) reconciled before checkout.
  • The system must scale elastically for seasonal spikes without manual capacity changes.
  • Cart data must be secured and scoped so users can only access their own carts.
  • The system must handle concurrent updates to the same cart from multiple devices without losing changes.
  • Cost per stored cart should be optimized given many idle/abandoned carts.

Assumptions & Out of Scope

  • Payment processing, order fulfillment, and inventory reservation are handled by downstream services and out of scope.
  • Pricing and promotion computation logic is assumed to be provided by a pricing service.
  • Product catalog details and search are out of scope; the cart references product identifiers.
  • Assume authentication and user identity are provided by an existing platform service.
