Netflix — Requirements Exercise

Design brief: Design a global video-on-demand streaming service that lets users browse a catalog and stream movies and TV shows on demand across many device types.

Functional Requirements

  • Users can browse and search a catalog of movies and shows by title, genre, cast, and other attributes.
  • Users can stream video on demand and start playback within a couple of seconds of pressing play.
  • The system must adapt streaming quality to the viewer's available bandwidth and device capability.
  • Users can pause, seek, and resume, including resuming a title from where they left off on any device.
  • The system must provide personalized recommendations and home-page rows per user profile.
  • Users can maintain multiple profiles per account, each with its own history and watchlist.
  • Users can add titles to a watchlist and rate or mark content.
  • The system must support subtitles and multiple audio tracks/languages per title.
  • The system must enforce concurrent-stream limits and content licensing/geographic availability per region.
  • The system must support downloading titles for offline viewing on mobile devices.
  • The system must ingest and transcode newly published titles into multiple renditions before they go live.

Non-Functional Requirements

  • Support 200M+ subscribers and tens of millions of concurrent viewers at peak.
  • Deliver video with startup latency under ~2 seconds and minimal rebuffering (target buffer ratio well under 1%).
  • Serve traffic globally with low latency regardless of the viewer's region.
  • Availability target of 99.99% for the playback and catalog-browsing paths.
  • Sustain very high egress throughput (petabytes per day) during peak evening hours.
  • Playback experience (browse, recommendations) should feel responsive with UI responses under ~300 ms at p99.
  • Content and user data must be secured with DRM for licensed video and encrypted transport.
  • The system must be read-heavy and elastic to absorb regional peaks and new-release spikes.
  • Delivery cost per streamed hour should be optimized, favoring caching content close to viewers.
  • Viewing history and resume points must be durable and consistent enough to work across devices.

Assumptions & Out of Scope

  • Content production, licensing negotiation, and the recommendation ML model internals are out of scope.
  • Assume billing, subscription tiers, and authentication are handled by existing services.
  • Live/linear broadcast streaming is excluded; scope is on-demand catalog playback.
  • Assume a third-party or owned CDN capacity is available for last-mile delivery.
