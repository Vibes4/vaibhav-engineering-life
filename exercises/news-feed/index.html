News Feed — Requirements Exercise

Design brief: Design a social news feed that shows each user a personalized, continuously updated stream of posts from accounts they follow.

Functional Requirements

  • Users can create posts containing text, images, and video, and edit or delete their own posts.
  • Users can follow and unfollow other users and pages.
  • The system must generate a feed of posts from followed accounts for each user.
  • Users can scroll the feed with pagination and load older entries on demand.
  • Users can like, comment on, and share posts, with counts reflected in the feed.
  • The system must rank feed entries by relevance rather than strict reverse-chronological order.
  • New posts from followed accounts must appear in the feed shortly after publication.
  • Users can mute, hide, or report individual posts or accounts.
  • The system must deduplicate reshared content and avoid showing the same post repeatedly.
  • The system must support notifications for interactions on a user's own posts.
  • Users can view any account's profile and its list of posts.

Non-Functional Requirements

  • Support 300M daily active users with an aggregate feed-read rate of ~1M requests per second at peak.
  • Feed load must return the first screen within 200 ms at p95.
  • A newly published post should become eligible for followers' feeds within 5 seconds.
  • The system must tolerate celebrity accounts with tens of millions of followers without degrading fan-out.
  • Read availability must be 99.99%; a brief posting outage is more tolerable than a feed-read outage.
  • Feed content may be eventually consistent; slightly stale counts are acceptable.
  • Media and post data must be durable with no permanent loss of published content.
  • User privacy settings (private accounts, blocked users) must always be enforced on reads.
  • Read-heavy workload assumes roughly a 100:1 read-to-write ratio; cost must scale sub-linearly with reads.

Assumptions & Out of Scope

  • Assume authentication, user registration, and the social graph store already exist.
  • Assume media upload, transcoding, and CDN delivery are provided by a separate media service.
  • Direct messaging, stories, and live video are out of scope.
  • Ad insertion and monetization ranking are excluded from this exercise.
  • Assume content moderation of illegal material is handled by a dedicated trust-and-safety pipeline.
