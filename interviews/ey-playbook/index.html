EY GDS — Interview Playbook Senior Cloud Native Developer (AWS)

Role: Senior Cloud Native Developer — EY GDS Technology Consulting. Stack the JD actually names: Node.js / TypeScript, AWS (EC2, EKS, Lambda, S3, RDS, DynamoDB, VPC, IAM, SGs, API Gateway, SNS, SQS), IaC (CloudFormation, Terraform), and CI/CD + testing + security + cost as secondary weightings. Band: 4–8 years. One 60-minute slot.

The single most important framing: this is a consulting delivery role, not a product-engineering role. Every answer should end in a trade-off and a business consequence ("…which costs ~30% more but takes RPO from 15 minutes to near-zero, so I'd only do it if the client's data-loss tolerance justified it"). EY hires people who can defend a design in front of a client architect, not people who can recite service limits.

What a 60-minute panel actually looks like

EY GDS experienced-hire loops are typically 3 stages: a technical panel (this one), then a managerial/senior-manager round, then HR/fitment. Some candidates get a Glider/HackerRank assessment first. A single 60-minute technical slot almost always splits roughly like this:

MinutesWhat happensWhat they're scoring
0–5Your intro + "walk me through your current project"Can you narrate an architecture crisply? Do you own it or just work in it?
5–20Node.js / TypeScript depth — event loop, async, error handling, memoryIs the "senior" real, or framework-deep only?
20–40AWS breadth + depth — Lambda vs ECS/EKS, SQS vs SNS, RDS vs DynamoDB, VPC/IAMHave you actually run production on AWS, or just done tutorials?
40–52One open scenario — "design X" or "this is slow/failing, debug it"Structured thinking under ambiguity. This is the round-decider.
52–60CI/CD, IaC, security, cost — rapid fire; then your questionsBreadth across the SDLC; whether you think about money.
Watch out: because you "know AWS but have never been interviewed on it", your risk is not knowledge — it's shape. Untrained candidates answer AWS questions by listing features. Trained candidates answer with: definition → when I'd pick it → what breaks → what I'd pick instead. Four beats, 60–90 seconds. Drill the shape, not more facts.

The 4-beat answer template (use it for every AWS question)

  1. One-line definition — "SQS is a managed pull-based queue; one message goes to one consumer."
  2. When I reach for it — "I use it to decouple a spiky producer from a slow consumer, and to absorb backpressure."
  3. The sharp edge — "Standard is at-least-once and unordered, so consumers must be idempotent; visibility timeout must exceed max processing time or you get duplicate work."
  4. The alternative + why not — "If I needed fan-out to several independent consumers I'd put SNS or EventBridge in front; if I needed replay and ordered per-key history, Kinesis or MSK."
Say this when you don't know something: "I haven't run that in production. What I'd do is check X, and my working assumption would be Y — is that the direction?" Senior candidates are allowed gaps; they are not allowed bluffing. EY panels probe, and a fabricated answer that collapses on the second question costs more than the admission.

Your 90-second opening (script it, don't improvise)

Structure: years + stack → current system in one sentence → scale numbers → your specific ownership → why EY. Numbers are what separate senior from mid. Fill these in from your own work before the call:

Say this: "I'm a backend engineer with ~N years, primarily Node.js and TypeScript on AWS. Currently I work on <system> — a <microservices / event-driven> platform handling roughly <X> requests a day with <Y> services. I own <2–3 specific services> end-to-end: API design, the data model on <RDS/DynamoDB>, the async pipeline on <SQS/SNS/EventBridge>, plus the CI/CD pipeline and the CloudWatch alarms that page us. The piece I'm proudest of is <one concrete win with a number — latency cut, cost cut, incident class eliminated>. I'm looking at EY GDS because the work is client-facing modernization across many domains rather than one product, and that's the range I want next."

Have three stories loaded and rehearsed — you will be asked for at least two of them:

  • A production incident you debugged — symptom, how you narrowed it, root cause, the fix, and the prevention you added afterwards. The prevention is the senior part.
  • A design decision with a real trade-off — including what you gave up and what you'd do differently now.
  • A disagreement — with an architect, a PM, or a client. Resolution by evidence, not by seniority.

Gaps to pre-empt honestly

The JD mentions a few things you may not have touched. Do not pretend. Pre-empt them with a one-line honest frame plus adjacent experience — this reads as confidence, not weakness:

  • EKS/Kubernetes — if your container experience is ECS/Fargate, say so: "I've run production on ECS Fargate; I know the K8s object model (deployments, services, ingress, probes, HPA) but haven't operated a cluster. Most of what I own transfers — the scheduling and networking concepts are the same, the operational surface is bigger."
  • Terraform vs CloudFormation — if you only know one, say which, and demonstrate you understand the concepts (declarative desired state, drift, state locking, modules) rather than the syntax.
  • "COFFEE, MQ" in the JD — COFFEE is an internal/client-specific integration platform; MQ is IBM MQ or Amazon MQ. Nobody expects you to know COFFEE. If asked: "I haven't used COFFEE; on the messaging side I've worked with <SQS/SNS/Kafka/RabbitMQ>, and the primitives — queues, topics, durable delivery, poison-message handling — carry over."

Questions to ask them (have 4 ready, ask 2–3)

  • "Is this role on one long-running client engagement, or rotating across accounts? What's the typical engagement length?"
  • "How are the teams split between GDS and the onshore client team — do GDS engineers own services end-to-end including on-call, or is it build-and-hand-over?"
  • "What does the current AWS estate look like for the account I'd join — mostly serverless, mostly containers, or still mid-migration from on-prem?"
  • "Where does this team sit on IaC maturity today — is everything Terraform/CloudFormation, or is there still console-managed infrastructure to bring under control?"
  • "What separates someone who does well here in year one from someone who struggles?"
Don't ask about salary, band, or WFH policy in the technical round — that's the HR round. Asking early reads as disengaged from the work.

The 48-hour prep order

  1. Rapid revision sheet — skim it first to find your weak areas (it's the last page in this section).
  2. AWS sheets — Compute & Storage, Data, Networking & Security, Integration. These are ~40% of the round.
  3. Scenario sheet — read every scenario out loud. This is the round-decider and cannot be crammed silently.
  4. Node.js sheet — you likely know this; skim for the phrasing, not the content.
  5. Microservices, IaC/CI-CD, Well-Architected — one pass each.
  6. Behavioural — write your three stories out in full sentences. Do not wing these.
